# Methodical: rules

Four ways to play, from a 10-minute classroom warm-up to a 45-minute game for a lab group.
All of them use the same cards.

## Components

| Deck | Cards | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Question** | 74 | A research problem with a field, a **suit** (the kind of question) and **constraint** icons. |
| **Design** | 60 | A study type, colour-coded by **family**. It shows which suits it serves, which constraints it fits or struggles with, and which threats it is weak against. |
| **Threat** | 29 | A bias or validity threat, with a real example. This is the "fallacy" deck. |
| **Safeguard** | 34 | A design or analysis feature that counters specific threats, usable only with certain design families. This is the "refutation" deck. |
| **Twist** | 17 | A mid-round surprise that adds a constraint ("The ethics board says no") or triggers an event. |
| **Research Programme** | 6 | A big challenge broken into 5 stages, printed as an A6 mat for the cooperative mode. |
| **Reference** | 4 | Suits, constraints, families and scoring. |
| **Answer key** | booklet | Best / defensible / trap answers for every Question and Programme, with the reasoning. |

All cards share one back, so a face-down deck never reveals anything. The answers are in the answer-key booklet
(`print/answer-key.html`), and the solo app shows them too.

You also need a pen and paper for scores, and (for modes 1 and 2) a scrap of paper or a small whiteboard per player.

### Reading the cards

Every card is laid out like a trading card:

- **Name bar** with the card's **attribute** (for Designs and Questions, its main suit).
- **Level row**: a Design's suits plus its **cost** (gold coins) and **time** (white diamonds); a Question's **difficulty** (★).
- **Art box**: on a Design card the picture *is* the study's diagram, e.g. a die splitting a crowd into two arms, or the jump at a cutoff.
- **Text box**: a type line, one sentence of effect text, then icon rows: ✔ constraints the design fits, ✘ constraints it struggles with,
  and the threats it is weak against.

**Suits** (the kind of question):
📊 Describe · 🧭 Explore · 🔗 Associate · 🎯 Cause · 🔮 Predict · 📏 Measure · 🧮 Explain · 🛠️ Build · 📚 Synthesize

**Constraints** (the situation):
🚫 Can't assign · 🔎 Rare outcome · ☢️ Rare exposure · ⏳ Long latency · ⚡ Brief trigger · 🗄️ Existing data ·
1️⃣ One unit · 👥 Group-level · ✂️ Cutoff rule · 📅 Rollout · 💸 Tight budget · ❓ Uncharted · 🗂️ Many studies

The quickest way to match a question to a design is to check that the design **serves the question's suit**,
**fits** its constraints, and doesn't **struggle** with any of them. The icons are a help, not the answer.
Two designs can both pass the icon check, and choosing between them is where the thinking happens.

### Scoring (all competitive modes)

| Your design is… | Points |
|---|---|
| 🥇 **Best** (gold in the answer key) | **+3** |
| 🥈 **Defensible** (silver) | **+1** |
| ⚠️ **Trap** (listed with the reason it fails) | **−2** |
| Unlisted | **0**, but you may **appeal** |

**Appeals.** The answer key is not scripture. If your design is unlisted, you have 60 seconds to argue for it.
The other players vote (the reader breaks ties). If the appeal is upheld, you score **+1**.
Good appeals are the whole point of the game, so write the strong ones down and send them back as improvements to the deck.

## Mode 1: Methods Map (2–30 players, 10–20 min, learning)

Good for classes, journal clubs and first games.

1. **Lay out the Methods Map.** Place all 60 Design cards face up, grouped by family colour.
2. A reader draws a **Question** card and reads it aloud, including its constraints.
3. Everyone silently writes down the design they'd choose. Advanced players also write the **one threat** they'd worry about most.
4. Reveal together. The reader looks the card up in the answer-key booklet and reads the answer and the *why* aloud.
5. Score as above. If the advanced variant is used, +1 for naming the card's "Watch out for" threat.
6. Play 10 questions, or set a time limit. Highest score wins.

*Teams variant:* with large groups, play in teams of 3–4 that must agree on one design. The discussion inside each team is where most of the learning happens.

## Mode 2: Peer Review (3–6 players, 30–45 min, competitive)

You play scientists proposing designs and reviewers attacking them.

**Setup.** Lay out the Methods Map as in Mode 1. Shuffle the Threat and Safeguard decks and deal each player
**4 Threats** and **4 Safeguards**. Shuffle the Questions and Twists.

**Each round** one player is the **Reviewer** (rotate clockwise). Everyone else is an **Author**.

1. **Call.** The Reviewer draws a Question and reads it aloud.
2. **Propose.** Authors secretly write a design, then reveal at once. The Reviewer checks the answer key and scores each Author (best / defensible / trap / appeal).
3. **Twist** *(optional, recommended after your first game).* The Reviewer flips a Twist card.
   - If it **adds a constraint** that an Author's design *struggles* with, that Author must either **switch** to another design (scored afresh against the original key, −1 for the switch) or **argue** (60 s, table vote) that the design still works. A lost argument costs 1 point.
   - If it is an **event**, apply it.
4. **Review.** The Reviewer may play **one Threat against each Author**. The Threat must be on that design's *Weak vs* list, unless the Reviewer convinces the table in one sentence that it applies anyway.
5. **Rebut.** The attacked Author may play **one Safeguard** that *counters* that Threat and is *usable with* the design's family.
   - Successful rebuttal: **Author +1**.
   - No rebuttal: **Reviewer +1**, **Author −1**.
6. **Refill.** Discard played cards. Everyone draws back up to 4 Threats and 4 Safeguards.

**End.** When every player has been Reviewer twice, the highest score is **funded**. Ties go to the player with fewer trap answers.

> **Design note.** Threats and safeguards are matched by explicit lists so that the game runs without an expert at the table.
> The "convince the table" escape valve keeps it honest for expert players who can see subtler attacks.

## Mode 3: Research Programme (2–5 players, 30–45 min, cooperative)

Real science is a sequence of studies, not a single one. Here the team runs a five-stage programme against the clock.

**Setup.** Draw one **Research Programme** mat. Lay out the Methods Map. Shuffle Threats and Twists.
Deal the team a shared hand of **6 Safeguards**. Set a **Doubt** counter to 0.

**For each of the 5 stages**, in order:

1. Read the stage question. The team agrees on **one design** (2 minutes, majority vote if stuck).
2. Check the programme's entry in the answer key. A listed design advances. An unlisted design can advance if the team writes
   a one-sentence justification that a neutral player (or the rulebook owner) accepts; otherwise add **+1 Doubt**.
3. Flip a **Twist**. If it adds a constraint the chosen design struggles with, add **+1 Doubt**, or spend one Safeguard
   and explain how it rescues the design.
4. Flip a **Threat**. If it's on the design's Weak vs list, counter it with a usable Safeguard from the shared hand
   or add **+1 Doubt**. If it doesn't apply, discard it.
5. Draw 1 Safeguard into the shared hand.

**Win** by completing all five stages with **Doubt ≤ 4**. At 5 Doubt the programme is **retracted** and you lose.
Scale difficulty with the number of Safeguards dealt (8 = easy, 4 = hard).

## Mode 4: Reviewer 2 (2+ players, 10 min, drill)

A fast critique drill.

1. Flip a random **Question** and a random **Design**. Most pairs will be mismatched, which is the point.
2. The first player to slap the table names the **single strongest reason it fails** (or argues that it works).
   If the table agrees, they take the Question card.
3. The first player to collect 5 Question cards wins.

Variant *"Defend the indefensible"*: the player must argue **for** the mismatched design, and must name every
Safeguard they'd need. It teaches that most designs can be rescued, at a price.

## Solo play

Open `app/index.html` in a browser (no internet needed). It deals you questions, lets you pick from the full Methods Map,
explains the answer, and then asks which safeguard counters the key threat. Your stats stay in your browser.

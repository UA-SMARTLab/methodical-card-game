Experimental / Method

Adaptive / platform trial

Which of many treatments work, found out as fast as the data allow?

One master protocol tests many treatments against a shared control; arms are dropped or added as data accrue.

How it works

  1. One protocol

    One master protocol runs several treatment arms side by side, all compared with one shared control arm.

    “a common control for multiple experimental arms can increase the efficiency of the platform trial and make it more appealing to participants and sponsors.”

    Lu et al. (2021), Consideration of control

  2. Write the rules

    Before any data come in, write down what may change and the rules that will trigger each change.

    “A clinical trial design that offers pre-planned opportunities to use accumulating trial data to modify aspects of an ongoing trial while preserving the validity and integrity of that trial.”

    Dimairo et al. (2020), box 1, definition of an adaptive design

  3. Look in between

    At planned moments, analyse the data gathered so far.

    “A statistical analysis or review of accumulating data from an ongoing trial (interim data) to inform trial adaptations (before the final analysis), which may or may not involve treatment group comparisons.”

    Dimairo et al. (2020), box 2, interim analysis

  4. Drop and add

    Following the rules, stop arms that have their answer and open new ones, without starting a new trial.

    “‘Platform trials’ were defined as any clinical trials that allowed the intervention arm(s) to be dropped and the flexibility of introducing new intervention(s) during the trial.”

    Park et al. (2019), table 1, study designs

    “Although the randomization of patients to receive dexamethasone, hydroxychloroquine, or lopinavir–ritonavir has now been stopped, the trial continues randomization to groups receiving azithromycin, tocilizumab, or convalescent plasma.”

    RECOVERY Collaborative Group (2021), Methods, trial design and oversight

  5. Compare like with like

    Compare a new arm with control patients randomised at the same time; patients from before it opened may differ.

    “A general consensus is to only include the concurrent randomized control data in the comparison with the corresponding experimental arm for confirmatory trials with registrations purposes”

    Lu et al. (2021), Consideration of control

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • History & secular trends

    Something else changed at the same time.

    A comparison group living through the same period.

    “It is also possible that due to potential changes in medical practice, there may be drift in population that results in better or worse outcomes for the control arm over time.”

    Lu et al. (2021), Consideration of control

  • Forking paths & p-hacking

    Try enough analyses and one of them will be 'significant'.

    Lock in the plan before you see the data.

    “It involves pre-specifying a set of actions guiding how decisions about implementing the trial adaptations are made given interim observed data (decision rules).”

    Dimairo et al. (2020), box 2, adaptive decision-making criteria

A real example

RECOVERY (COVID-19): dexamethasone shown to save lives within about 100 days of launch.

Defining reference: Woodcock & LaVange (2017). Master Protocols to Study Multiple Therapies, Multiple Diseases, or Both. New England Journal of Medicine.

Delphi consensus

What do experts agree on where evidence is thin?

Experts rate statements anonymously over repeated rounds, seeing the group's feedback, until consensus emerges.

How it works

  1. Choose the panel

    Decide in advance who counts as an expert for this question, then invite people who meet those criteria.

    “The panel member selection is undoubtedly the most crucial aspect of Delphi research studies”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Panel members”

    “The selection of a panel or voting members in a Delphi study should be based on objective and predefined criteria and related to the problem under study.”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Panel members”, evaluation point

  2. Rate anonymously

    Each expert rates every statement alone, usually on a numbered scale. Nobody knows who gave which rating, so no one can dominate.

    “The anonymity of individual members in a Delphi study removes the inherent bias like dominance and group conformity (defined as groupthink) observed with face-to-face group meetings.”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Introduction”

  3. Feed back

    After each round, send everyone the group's results (median, spread, anonymous comments) and let them reconsider.

    “After each of the survey rounds, obtained data are analyzed and presented in an easily interpretable format to all the panel experts.”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Delphi rounds”

    “Controlled feedback gives insight to the individual member about the trend and one can change its response if needed.”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Delphi rounds”

  4. Repeat the rounds

    Rate again, round after round. Consensus is judged by a rule fixed beforehand, such as a share of experts agreeing.

    “The consensus definition used commonly is the percentage of agreement based on a predefined cut-off, central tendency, or a combination of both. However, percentage agreement varies widely from 50%-97% and is selected arbitrarily”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Closing criteria”, “Consensus”

  5. Stop when stable

    Stop when the answers no longer change from one round to the next, by a stopping rule written down before the first round.

    “Stability is defined as the consistency of responses between successive rounds of a study”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Closing criteria”, “Stability”

    “The criteria for stopping the Delphi rounds based on consensus or stability should be identified a priori.”

    Nasa et al. (2021), “Closing criteria”, evaluation point

Watch the rounds converge

“consensus can be there in unstable responses, and stability can be there without consensus”

Nasa et al. (2021), “Closing criteria”, “Stability”

Stable means the share rating 7–9 changed by 5 points or less and the interquartile range did not change.

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Selection bias

    Who ends up in the sample depends on what you're studying.

    Sample from the whole population, not the convenient part.

  • Researcher bias

    You see what you expected to see.

    Write down your own lens, and let a second coder check.

A real example

COMET-initiative 'core outcome sets' that tell trialists what every trial in a field should measure.

Defining reference: Dalkey & Helmer (1963). An Experimental Application of the DELPHI Method to the Use of Experts. Management Science.

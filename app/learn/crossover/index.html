Experimental / Method

Crossover trial

Which of two treatments works better, compared within each person?

Each participant gets both treatments in random order, separated by a washout. Needs far fewer people.

How it works

  1. Randomise the order

    Chance decides the order in which each person gets the treatments: A then B, or B then A. Everyone gets both.

    “each individual in a crossover trial receives multiple interventions but in a random order; that is, participants are randomised to sequences of interventions.”

    Dwan et al. (2019), Methodological features of randomised crossover trials

    “The allocation might be to sequence one, in which participants have A followed by B, or to sequence two, in which participants have B followed by A.”

    Dwan et al. (2019), item 8a

  2. First period

    Each person takes their first treatment for a set time, and the outcome is measured at the end of it.

    “Period: a length of time when one treatment was received.”

    Dwan et al. (2019), box 1, glossary

  3. Washout

    A gap with no treatment lets the first one wear off, so it does not leak into the second period.

    “Washout: a length of time between treatment periods when no treatment is received to allow the treatment to wear off.”

    Dwan et al. (2019), box 1, glossary

  4. Swap

    Each person now gets the other treatment, for the same length of time, measured the same way.

    “crossover trials provide each participant with two or more sequential treatments in a random order usually separated by a washout period”

    Mills et al. (2009), Introduction

  5. Compare within

    Compare each person's result on A with their own result on B. Differences between people drop out, which is why far fewer people are needed.

    “Within a trial, each participant is able to act as his or her own control and permits between and within group comparisons”

    Mills et al. (2009), Introduction

    “The analysis of a crossover trial should respect the within participant nature of the comparisons.”

    Dwan et al. (2019), item 12a

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Carryover

    The first treatment lingers into the second period.

    Let the first treatment wear off.

    “the effect of the first intervention persists into the second period so that the observed difference between the treatments depends on the order in which they were received”

    Dwan et al. (2019), Methodological features of randomised crossover trials

  • Attrition & missing data

    The people who drop out are not like the people who stay.

    Analyse everyone as randomized, whatever happened next.

    “Patients may drop out after the first intervention period and thus not receive a second or third treatment. This makes within-subject comparison impossible”

    Mills et al. (2009), Discussion

A real example

Bioequivalence studies of generic versus brand-name drugs.

Defining reference: Mills et al. (2009). Design, analysis, and presentation of crossover trials. Trials.

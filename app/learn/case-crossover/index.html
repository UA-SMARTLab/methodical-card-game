Analytical observational / Method

Case-crossover

Does a brief exposure trigger a sudden event soon afterwards?

Each case is their own control: compare exposure just before the event with the same person's exposure at other times.

How it works

  1. Cases only

    Take only people who had the event. Nobody without the event is needed.

    “A case-crossover study only includes individuals who experience an event (known as cases).”

    Lewer et al. (2022), Introduction

  2. Hazard window

    Mark the short window just before the event, as long as the exposure could plausibly act.

    “we compared phone activity during the hazard interval (time immediately before the crash) with phone activity during control intervals (equivalent times during which participants were driving but did not crash) in the previous week”

    Vandenbroucke et al. (2007), item 4, example

  3. Control windows

    Pick earlier windows in the same person: far enough from the event not to be affected by it, near enough to be comparable.

    “The referent windows should be far enough from the event so that exposure is not affected by the event. Simultaneously, referent windows should be sufficiently recent that the underlying rate of exposure is comparable, or exchangeable.”

    Lewer et al. (2022), section Choosing the right control

  4. Compare within

    For each person, compare exposure in the hazard window with exposure in the control windows. Only people whose exposure differs between windows carry information.

    “Each participant serves as their own control, and the analysis tests whether exposure times are associated with outcome times within individuals.”

    Lewer et al. (2022), Introduction

    “Case-crossover studies use information from cases only if the exposure status varies over time.”

    Lewer et al. (2022), section Positives and pitfalls

  5. What cancels

    Anything about the person that does not change between windows cancels out, measured or not. Things that change from hour to hour do not.

    “a strength of the case-crossover design is that it eliminates time invariant confounders, even when unmeasured.”

    Lewer et al. (2022), section Positives and pitfalls

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Recall bias

    Cases remember their past differently from controls.

    Use instruments proven to measure what matters.

  • History & secular trends

    Something else changed at the same time.

    A comparison group living through the same period.

    “If the event does not affect subsequent exposure, such as in studies of air pollution, then referent windows both before and after the event can reduce the risk of bias owing to time trends in the exposure.”

    Lewer et al. (2022), section Choosing the right control

A real example

Mittleman et al. (1993): heavy exertion as a trigger of heart attack within the hour.

Defining reference: Maclure (1991). The Case-Crossover Design: A Method for Studying Transient Effects on the Risk of Acute Events. American Journal of Epidemiology.

Qualitative & mixed / Method

Interviews & thematic analysis

How and why do people experience this, in their own words?

In-depth interviews or focus groups analysed for patterns of meaning. Answers 'how' and 'why' from people's own view.

How it works

  1. Interview

    Talk with people one to one or in focus groups, with open questions, and record what they say. Choose them because they have lived the topic.

    “An organized and rigorous approach to analyzing qualitative data can yield important insights into the intricacies of human behavior and can answer “how” and “why” questions.”

    Coates et al. (2021), Abstract

  2. Transcribe and read

    Turn each recording into a transcript, check it against the recording, remove names, and read it closely.

    “The researcher read the transcripts and made corrections, including clarifying/correcting data collected”

    Coates et al. (2021), “Data collection”

  3. Code

    Mark each passage that says something about the question with a short label, a code.

    “A “code” is a short representation that captures the salient features of a main idea expressed by subjects in their responses to questions in a qualitative interview study.”

    Coates et al. (2021), “Subjective data”

  4. Agree a codebook

    Write down what each code means, with examples. Where two coders differ, they talk it through and settle it.

    “It is helpful to create a guide, or “codebook,” that indicates what each code represents, what ideas should be included in it, and examples from the raw data.”

    Coates et al. (2021), “Subjective data”

    “a simple discussion allows each person to explain their thinking and arrive at a mutually acceptable decision.”

    Coates et al. (2021), “Resolution of disputes”

  5. Build themes

    Gather codes that share a meaning into themes, and themes into subthemes where people differ.

    “After successfully coding the transcripts, the researchers can look for ideas that are similar and designate these as discrete themes”

    Coates et al. (2021), “Identification of themes and subthemes”

  6. Interpret

    Say what the themes mean for the question you asked, illustrated with people's own words.

    “After organizing the data into themes and subthemes, the researchers must interpret the meaning within the context of the research question.”

    Coates et al. (2021), “Interpreting themes to engender change”

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Researcher bias

    You see what you expected to see.

    Write down your own lens, and let a second coder check.

  • Thin data

    Too few voices to support the story.

    Keep sampling until nothing new turns up.

  • Selection bias

    Who ends up in the sample depends on what you're studying.

    Sample from the whole population, not the convenient part.

A real example

Exploring why patients stop taking a medication they were prescribed.

Defining reference: Braun & Clarke (2006). Using thematic analysis in psychology. Qualitative Research in Psychology.

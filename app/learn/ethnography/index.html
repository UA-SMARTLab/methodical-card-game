Qualitative & mixed / Method

Ethnography

How do these people see and do things, from the inside?

Immerse yourself in a setting for months, observing and taking part, to understand practices and meanings from the inside.

How it works

  1. Get in

    Prepare, then win access to the group. A trusted member, a key informant, opens doors an outsider cannot.

    “they must be accepted temporarily within the culture in order to gather accurate information.”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Limitations of ethnography”

    “Additionally, the development of key informants is essential.”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Limitations of ethnography”

  2. Observe and take part

    Live and work alongside the people you study, in their own setting, and write field notes on what you see and hear. Interviews add their own words.

    “Ethnographies are normally conducted in a single setting, and data collection is largely dependent upon participant observation and interviews.”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Introduction”

  3. Stay long

    Stay for months, not days. Only long exposure lets you describe the setting richly and see past the show put on for a visitor.

    “ethnography is a research method characterized by long-term fieldwork since thick description of the participants and setting may only be acquired from sufficient exposure to them.”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Introduction”

  4. Follow the leads

    Let the field change the plan. A conversation raises a question you did not expect, and you go after it.

    “In ethnography, data collection can sometimes change along the course of the study. A conversation with one study participant may lead to generation of further questions.”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Uses of ethnography”, the Perry et al. example

  5. Interpret through culture

    Read your notes through the group's own culture and history, and write an account that its members would recognise.

    “presenting an accurate reflection of participant perspectives and behaviors”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Introduction”, characteristic 3 of LeCompte and Schensul's seven

    “using the concept of culture as a lens through which to interpret study results”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Introduction”, characteristic 7

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Researcher bias

    You see what you expected to see.

    Write down your own lens, and let a second coder check.

    “Subjectivity is certainly a limitation of ethnography. The interpretation of the cultural experience will vary among researchers.”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Limitations of ethnography”

  • Limited generalizability

    True here, but maybe not anywhere else.

    Run it where the results will be used.

    “It is difficult to generalize with the ethnography method.”

    Goodson & Vassar (2011), “Limitations of ethnography”

  • Expectation effects

    Knowing who got what changes the result.

    Write down your own lens, and let a second coder check.

A real example

Whyte's Street Corner Society (1943); Latour & Woolgar's Laboratory Life (1979).

Defining reference: Reeves et al. (2008). Qualitative research methodologies: ethnography. BMJ.

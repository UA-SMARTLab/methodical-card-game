Measurement / Method

Instrument development & validation

Does this questionnaire measure what it claims to?

Build a questionnaire or scale from theory and input, then test its structure, reliability and validity (psychometrics).

How it works

  1. Define the construct

    Write down exactly what the scale should capture, and how it relates to neighbouring ideas, before writing a single item.

    “A critical first step is to develop a precise and detailed conception of the target construct and its theoretical context.”

    Clark & Watson (1995), p. 310

  2. Write too many items

    Draft a broad pool of items, more than you will keep, covering the construct and its edges, each worded with care.

    “the content of the initial item pool should be overinclusive and item wording needs careful attention.”

    Clark & Watson (1995), abstract, p. 309

  3. Test widely

    Give the pool, with measures of related constructs, to a varied sample that spans the whole population the scale is for.

    “the item pool should be tested, along with variables that assess closely related constructs, on a heterogeneous sample representing the entire range of the target population.”

    Clark & Watson (1995), abstract, p. 309

  4. Keep one dimension

    Keep the items that measure one thing together, using factor analysis, not those that merely push internal consistency up.

    “in selecting scale items, the goal is unidimensionality rather than internal consistency; this means that virtually all interitem correlations should be moderate in magnitude.”

    Clark & Watson (1995), abstract, p. 309

    “Factor analysis can play a crucial role in ensuring the unidimensionality and discriminant validity of scales.”

    Clark & Watson (1995), abstract, p. 309

  5. Check the pattern

    The finished scale should correlate with what theory says it should, and not with what it should not.

    “a good measure will have a predicted convergent and discriminant correlational pattern”

    Clark & Watson (1995), p. 311

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Wrong construct

    You measured a proxy, not the real thing.

    Use instruments proven to measure what matters.

    “A primary goal of scale development is to create a valid measure of an underlying construct.”

    Clark & Watson (1995), abstract, p. 309

  • Selection bias

    Who ends up in the sample depends on what you're studying.

    Sample from the whole population, not the convenient part.

A real example

Development of the Maslach Burnout Inventory (1981).

Defining reference: Clark & Watson (1995). Constructing validity: Basic issues in objective scale development. Psychological Assessment.

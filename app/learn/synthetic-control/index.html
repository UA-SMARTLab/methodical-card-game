Quasi-experimental / Method

Synthetic control

What would have happened to this one place without the intervention?

Build a weighted 'twin' of one treated unit from untreated units that tracked it before the intervention; compare after.

How it works

  1. Pick the donors

    List untreated units that resemble the treated one in what drives the outcome, and leave out any that had a similar intervention.

    “It is essential for the credibility of the method that the donor pool only contains units that are similar to the treated unit in aspects important to the outcome”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Steps in conducting an SCM study

  2. Weight a twin

    Give each donor a weight between 0 and 1, the weights summing to 1, chosen so the weighted mix tracks the treated unit before the intervention.

    “A counterfactual SC is constructed by weighting control units, such that the level and trend of the preintervention SC most closely matches the treated unit.”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Box 2

    “The usual default is to limit the individual country weights to being between 0 and 1 (with the total of the weights being 1)”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Box 2

  3. Check the fit

    The twin must follow the treated unit closely for a long stretch before the intervention. If it can't, the method has nothing to stand on.

    “the credibility of the result relies on achieving a good preimplementation fit for the outcome of interest between treated unit and synthetic control, which is difficult if the treated unit is an outlier.”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Advantages, assumptions and limitations of SCM

  4. Read the gap

    Keep the weights fixed and run the twin forward. The gap between the real unit and its twin after the intervention is the estimated effect.

    “The difference between this counterfactual and the actual trend for the treated unit represents the estimated intervention effect.”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Box 2

  5. Run placebos

    Pretend each donor was treated and build its twin too. A real effect should make the treated unit's gap stand out from theirs.

    “Placebo analysis involves performing the analysis as if other units in the donor pool were the treated unit to generate a distribution of effect estimates.”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Steps in conducting an SCM study

Build the twin

“Once the weighting of the SC has been determined for the preintervention period, it is used to construct a counterfactual trend for the outcome in the postimplementation period.”

Bouttell et al. (2018), Box 2

One region started a programme in year 8; four others did not. Set the donors' weights by hand until the dashed twin follows the treated region before the programme, or let the page find the best fit. Then show the years after.

Example data, made up for this exercise: the treated region was built as half region A, 0.3 region B and 0.2 region C, with an effect growing by 1 a year from year 8.

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • History & secular trends

    Something else changed at the same time.

    A comparison group living through the same period.

    “there are no ‘shocks’, that is, other events that might differentially affect the outcome of interest in the treated unit or the potential control units in either the preintervention or postintervention period”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Advantages, assumptions and limitations of SCM

  • Forking paths & p-hacking

    Try enough analyses and one of them will be 'significant'.

    Lock in the plan before you see the data.

    “Clearly, the subjective aspects are important in this method.”

    Bouttell et al. (2018), Box 1

A real example

Abadie et al.: California's 1988 tobacco programme; the economic cost of German reunification.

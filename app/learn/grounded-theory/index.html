Grounded theory

What process explains what is going on here, built from the data up?

Collect and analyse data in iterative cycles (coding, comparing, sampling) to build a new theory from the ground up.

How it works

  1. Start small

    Begin with a few people chosen because they can speak to the question, and start analysing the first interview before doing the next.

    “Concurrent data generation and/or data collection and analysis is fundamental to GT research design.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “Purposive sampling”

  2. Code

    Break the data into small pieces and give each a short label that says what is happening in it.

    “The purpose of initial coding is to start the process of fracturing the data to compare incident to incident and to look for similarities and differences in beginning patterns in the data.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “Initial coding”

  3. Compare constantly

    Compare every new piece with the earlier ones, codes with codes, and gather them into categories that grow more abstract.

    “The initial stage of analysis compares incident to incident in each code. Initial codes are then compared to other codes. Codes are then collapsed into categories.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “Constant comparative analysis”

  4. Sample for theory

    Choose whom to see next by what the emerging categories still lack, not by who would make the sample representative.

    “Theoretical sampling in GT is for the development of a theoretical category, as opposed to sampling for population representation.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “Theoretical sampling”

  5. Write memos

    Write down, as you go, why you split, joined or renamed codes. The memos are the trail from data to theory.

    “Memos are the storehouse of ideas generated and documented through interacting with data.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “Memoing”

  6. Saturate and integrate

    Stop when new data no longer add to the categories. Then tie the categories together around a core into one theory.

    “Theoretical saturation ensues when new data analysis does not provide additional material to existing theoretical categories, and the categories are sufficiently explained.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “Intermediate coding”

    “The results of a GT study are communicated as a set of concepts, related to each other in an interrelated whole, and expressed in the production of a substantive theory.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “The grounded theory”

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Researcher bias

    You see what you expected to see.

    Write down your own lens, and let a second coder check.

  • Thin data

    Too few voices to support the story.

    Keep sampling until nothing new turns up.

    “During this process, additional information is sought to saturate categories under development.”

    Chun Tie et al. (2019), “Theoretical sampling”

A real example

Glaser & Strauss (1965), Awareness of Dying: how hospital staff and patients manage knowledge of death.

Defining reference: Glaser & Strauss (1967; 2017 edition). The discovery of grounded theory: strategies for qualitative research. Routledge.

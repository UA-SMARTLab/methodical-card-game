Prior art review

Has this already been made, published or claimed?

Search patents, standards, products, theses and papers for what already exists, before you build, publish or claim novelty.

How it works

  1. Search everywhere

    Prior art is anything the public could get at, not only papers. Search patents, standards, products, catalogues, theses and talks.

    “The state of the art shall be held to comprise everything made available to the public by means of a written or oral description, by use, or in any other way”

    EPC Art. 54 and 56, Art. 54(2)

    “patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public”

    35 U.S.C. § 102, § 102(a)(1)

  2. Check the date

    Only what was public before your filing date counts. US law excuses some of the inventor's own disclosures made up to a year before.

    “before the effective filing date of the claimed invention”

    35 U.S.C. § 102, § 102(a)(1)

    “A disclosure made 1 year or less before the effective filing date of a claimed invention shall not be prior art to the claimed invention under subsection (a)(1) if”

    35 U.S.C. § 102, § 102(b)(1)

  3. Is it new?

    Compare your idea with each earlier source, feature by feature. If one source already shows it, it is not new.

    “An invention shall be considered to be new if it does not form part of the state of the art.”

    EPC Art. 54 and 56, Art. 54(1)

  4. Is it obvious?

    Even if no single source shows it, ask whether a skilled person would have combined what was known to get there.

    “An invention shall be considered as involving an inventive step if, having regard to the state of the art, it is not obvious to a person skilled in the art.”

    EPC Art. 54 and 56, Art. 56

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Selection bias

    Who ends up in the sample depends on what you're studying.

    Search everywhere, including the drawers.

  • Researcher bias

    You see what you expected to see.

    Write down your own lens, and let a second coder check.

  • Publication bias

    The null results are still in the drawer.

    Search everywhere, including the drawers.

A real example

In re Hall (1986): one German thesis, shelved in a university library, counted as prior art against a US patent.

Defining reference: 35 U.S.C. § 102: Conditions for patentability; novelty. United States Code.

Evidence synthesis / Method

Network meta-analysis

Of all the treatments, which works best, even those never compared head to head?

Compare many treatments at once by combining direct and indirect evidence across a network of trials.

How it works

  1. Draw the network

    Collect every trial for the condition. Each treatment is a node; a line joins two treatments that some trial compared.

    “It is depicted by the use of nodes for each available treatment and links between the nodes when the respective treatments have been compared in 1 or more trials.”

    Ioannidis (2009), Treatment networks

  2. Read its shape

    Look at which comparisons were made often and which never. Gaps can reveal what sponsors chose to test.

    “Network geometry addresses what the shape of the network (nodes and links) looks like.”

    Ioannidis (2009), Network geometry

  3. Go via a common arm

    Two drugs never compared directly can still be compared through a treatment both were tested against, such as placebo.

    “To estimate the effect of bupropion versus that of nicotine replacement, we can use either information from their direct (head-to-head) comparison or information from the comparison of each medication against placebo.”

    Ioannidis (2009), Incoherence

  4. Check the loops

    Where a loop exists, compare the direct and the indirect estimate. If they disagree, find out why before combining.

    “Incoherence tells us whether the effect estimated from indirect comparisons differs from that estimated from direct comparisons.”

    Ioannidis (2009), Incoherence

  5. Estimate and rank

    Combine all the evidence into an effect for every pair, with its uncertainty, and the chance that each treatment is best.

    “A multiple treatments meta-analysis eventually derives treatment effects (e.g., relative risks) and the uncertainty (credibility intervals) for pair-wise comparisons of all of the treatments involved in the network, regardless of whether they were compared directly.”

    Ioannidis (2009), Multiple treatments meta-analysis

    “A useful feature is to rank the treatments on their probability of being the best.”

    Ioannidis (2009), Multiple treatments meta-analysis

Compare two drugs that never met

“Incoherence tells us whether the effect estimated from indirect comparisons differs from that estimated from direct comparisons.”

Ioannidis (2009), Incoherence

Drug A and drug B were each tested against placebo; one small trial also compared them head to head. Move the placebo-controlled results and see the indirect estimate of A versus B, its uncertainty, and whether it agrees with the direct trial.

Example data, made up for this exercise. On the log odds ratio scale the indirect estimate is (A vs placebo) minus (B vs placebo), and its variance is the sum of the two variances (the adjusted indirect comparison). Agreement is tested with a z test on the difference between direct and indirect.

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Apples & oranges

    Pooling apples with oranges.

    Plan in advance how you'll explain why studies differ.

  • Publication bias

    The null results are still in the drawer.

    Search everywhere, including the drawers.

A real example

Cipriani et al. (2018): 21 antidepressants ranked from 522 trials.

Defining reference: Lumley (2002). Network meta-analysis for indirect treatment comparisons. Statistics in Medicine.

Evidence synthesis / Method

Rapid review

What does the evidence say, in time for this decision?

A systematic review with transparently streamlined steps, delivered in weeks to inform an urgent decision.

How it works

  1. Agree the scope

    Work with the people who must decide: agree a narrow question and the deadline, and which steps of a full systematic review will be cut.

    “Rapid reviews are a form of knowledge synthesis in which components of the systematic review process are simplified or omitted to produce information in a timely manner”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Background

    “four papers suggested using integrated knowledge translation, in which researchers work closely with the knowledge users to complete the rapid review”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Results, Development papers on rapid reviews

  2. Narrow the search

    Search fewer sources and limit by date or language, knowing that each limit can miss studies.

    “limiting the literature search to published literature (24 %) or one database (2 %), limiting inclusion criteria by date (68 %) or language (49 %)”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Results, streamlined methods in the 82 rapid reviews

  3. One screens, one checks

    Instead of two reviewers screening independently, one screens and a second checks a part of the work.

    “According to the Cochrane Collaboration, all procedures including screening citations (titles and abstracts), screening full-text articles, data abstraction, and risk of bias appraisal, should be conducted by two individuals, independently”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Background

    “having one person screen and another verify or screen excluded studies (6 %), having one person abstract data and another verify (23 %)”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Results, streamlined methods in the 82 rapid reviews

  4. Summarise

    Most rapid reviews describe the findings in words rather than pooling them.

    “presenting results as a narrative summary (78 %)”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Results, streamlined methods in the 82 rapid reviews

    “none suggested conducting a meta-analysis as part of the rapid review.”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Results, Development papers on rapid reviews

  5. Report the shortcuts

    Say exactly which steps were cut, so readers can judge what the speed may have cost.

    “Thorough reporting of the methods is important because it is difficult to judge the bias of these reports without fully understanding what shortcuts were taken.”

    Tricco et al. (2015), Discussion

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Publication bias

    The null results are still in the drawer.

    Search everywhere, including the drawers.

  • Selection bias

    Who ends up in the sample depends on what you're studying.

    Search everywhere, including the drawers.

A real example

Cochrane Rapid Reviews informing COVID-19 policy in 2020.

Defining reference: Tricco et al. (2015). A scoping review of rapid review methods. BMC Medicine.

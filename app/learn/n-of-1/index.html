Experimental / Method

N-of-1 trial

Which treatment works best for this one patient?

Repeated, randomized, ideally blinded treatment periods within a single patient to find out what works for them.

How it works

  1. One patient

    The question is about one person, not the average patient: of two treatments, which helps them?

    “n-of-1 trials are within-patient, multi-period crossover trials designed to optimise the evaluation of health technologies in individual patients”

    Hawksworth et al. (2024), Background

  2. Randomise periods

    Treatment periods come in pairs, one of each treatment; chance decides the order inside each pair.

    “In these trials, a single patient receives one treatment for a period of time and then switches to receive another.”

    Hawksworth et al. (2024), Background

    “Usually, the order in which the patient receives the treatments is randomised.”

    Hawksworth et al. (2024), Background

  3. Blind

    Where possible, the treatments look the same, so the patient's hopes cannot colour the score.

    “The majority of studies blinded participants to treatment allocation (n = 57, 77.0%).”

    Hawksworth et al. (2024), Results, design characteristics

  4. Repeat

    Switch back and forth several times and measure in every period. One good week can be luck; the same winner pair after pair is not.

    “This “switching” is repeated, such that multiple measurements are obtained for each treatment.”

    Hawksworth et al. (2024), Background

    “The median (Q1, Q3) number of periods was six (4, 8). The median (Q1, Q3) period length was 14 days (5, 28).”

    Hawksworth et al. (2024), Results, design characteristics

  5. Decide

    The result answers the question for this patient: keep the treatment that won.

    “Such n-of-1 trials combine methodological rigour with the investigation of treatment effects in an individual patient to enable the determination of the optimal treatment for a particular patient, enabling individualised patient care”

    Hawksworth et al. (2024), Background

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Carryover

    The first treatment lingers into the second period.

    Let the first treatment wear off.

    “the effect of the first intervention persists into the second period so that the observed difference between the treatments depends on the order in which they were received”

    Dwan et al. (2019), Methodological features of randomised crossover trials

  • Expectation effects

    Knowing who got what changes the result.

    The control arm gets an identical-looking dummy.

A real example

Personalized trials of pain or ADHD medication in individual patients.

Defining reference: Guyatt et al. (1986). Determining Optimal Therapy — Randomized Trials in Individual Patients. New England Journal of Medicine.

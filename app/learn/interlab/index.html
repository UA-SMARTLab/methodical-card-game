Measurement / Method

Interlaboratory comparison

Do different laboratories get the same result from the same sample?

Identical samples go to many labs (a 'round robin'); differences reveal how reproducible a measurement method is.

How it works

  1. Prepare identical items

    A coordinator makes test items that are the same for everyone and ships them to each laboratory.

    “For each PT round two test items were prepared and distributed to the participants, consisting of a food or feed matrix containing or spiked with one or more GMOs.”

    Broothaerts et al. (2020), section 2.1

  2. Labs measure

    Each laboratory analyses its item with its usual method, without knowing the answer, and reports the result.

    “received the test items together with the instruction to identify the unknown GM event(s) present in the materials and to quantify the event(s) identified.”

    Broothaerts et al. (2020), section 2.1

  3. Set the target

    Fix the value the results are judged against: from how the item was made, from expert laboratories, from a certified reference, or from a robust mean of all results.

    “(i) from the formulation (mass fraction), (ii) by consensus of results reported by expert laboratories (using real-time or digital PCR), (iii) using the certified value of a certified reference material (CRM), or (iv) by calculating the robust mean of the results reported by the NRLs.”

    Broothaerts et al. (2020), section 2.2, ways of setting the assigned value

  4. Score each lab

    Compare each result with the target and score it. A lab with an unsatisfactory score looks for the cause.

    “The results reported by the NRLs were then compared to the assigned values and scored by using the percentage deviation”

    Broothaerts et al. (2020), section 2.3

    “NRLs that obtained an unsatisfactory z score were requested to perform a root-cause analysis”

    Broothaerts et al. (2020), section 1

  5. Read the spread

    The spread of all the labs' results shows how reproducible the method is between laboratories; repeated rounds show whether it improves.

    “The outcome revealed a gradual decrease of the relative standard deviation within the sets of the reported data with time.”

    Broothaerts et al. (2020), abstract

Where it breaks, and what protects it

  • Measurement error

    The instrument is noisy, biased or drifting.

    Use instruments proven to measure what matters.

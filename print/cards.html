Methodical: print and play

Every question deserves the right design. Version 0.1 (prototype). 186 cards in standard poker size (63 × 88 mm), 9 per A4 page, plus 6 research-programme mats (A6, 2 per page).

Printing. Print at 100% / actual size with background graphics on. Cut along the card edges. All cards share one back (backs.html): print it on the reverse of each sheet, or skip it and use opaque card sleeves, the easiest way to make a print-and-play deck shuffle like a real one.

Answers are in the separate answer-key booklet (answer-key.html), so the top card of a face-down deck never gives its answer away.

Suits: the question
  • Describe What is it? How much, how often, where, and is it changing?
  • Explore What is going on here? How and why do people experience or do it?
  • Associate Do X and Y go together, and does X come first?
  • Cause Does changing X change Y? Does the intervention work?
  • Predict What will happen, and to whom?
  • Measure Can we measure it accurately, reliably, and validly?
  • Explain What mechanism or law must hold? What follows from the theory?
  • Build Can we make something that works, and is the plan feasible?
  • Synthesize What does all the existing evidence say?
Constraints 1/2
  • Can't assign You can't (ethically or practically) decide who or what gets the exposure.
  • Rare outcome The outcome is rare: roughly 1 in 1,000 or rarer.
  • Rare exposure Only a small, identifiable group is exposed.
  • Long latency Years or decades pass between cause and effect.
  • Brief trigger The exposure is brief and its effect is immediate.
  • Existing data Records, registries, logs or archives already exist.
  • One unit Only one or a handful of units: one patient, one country, one factory.
Constraints 2/2
  • Group-level The exposure acts on whole groups, or would spill over between people.
  • Cutoff rule A threshold on a score decides who gets the exposure.
  • Rollout Everyone will get the intervention anyway; only the timing is flexible.
  • Tight budget Weeks or months, not years; a small budget.
  • Uncharted Little prior theory; you don't yet know which variables matter.
  • Many studies A substantial body of primary research already exists.

Screens & teen mental health

Is social media harming adolescents' mental health? Parents, platforms and governments want an answer.

  1. ExploreHow do teenagers themselves describe their social-media use and its effects?
  2. DescribeHow common are heavy use and depressive symptoms, and do they co-occur?
  3. AssociateDoes heavy use come before rising symptoms, or the other way round?
  4. CauseDoes cutting use for four weeks improve mood?
  5. SynthesizeWhat do all the trials and cohorts add up to?
Research programme · P2

Microplastics in our bodies

Microplastics turn up in blood, placentas and arteries. Are they a health problem?

  1. SynthesizeWhat has been studied so far, with which methods, and where are the gaps?
  2. MeasureDo different labs measuring the same tissue sample find the same particle counts?
  3. DescribeHow much do people carry, and does it vary by region and age?
  4. AssociateDo people with more microplastics in arterial plaque have more heart attacks later?
  5. ExplainThrough what cellular mechanism could particles cause inflammation, and at what dose?

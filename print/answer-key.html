Q01 City vape countCross-sectional surveyMonitoring / surveillanceProspective cohort
Case–control study		'What share, right now?' is a prevalence question, answered by one well-sampled snapshot. Put the effort into the sampling frame and weighting, not into follow-up. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q02 Tattoo rashCase report / case series–Randomized controlled trial
Systematic review & meta-analysis		A careful case series is the fastest way to raise a signal that others can test. Its job is to describe and alert. A case-control study is the logical next step, not the first. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q03 Vanishing butterfliesMonitoring / surveillance–Cross-sectional survey
Field experiment		Trends need standardized counts repeated over many years, the backbone of conservation science. Changes in observers, sites or methods over time are the main enemy. Watch out for: Measurement error.
Q04 Jupiter censusCross-sectional surveyMonitoring / surveillanceCase report / case series
Analytical / mathematical theory		It's a prevalence question in disguise: a defined sample of stars observed with a known detection completeness. Correcting for what the instrument can't see is the whole game. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q05 After approvalMonitoring / surveillanceRetrospective cohortRandomized controlled trial
Cross-sectional survey		Post-marketing pharmacovigilance (spontaneous reports plus active monitoring of health records) exists because rare harms are invisible in trials. Signals are then confirmed with analytical studies. Watch out for: Measurement error.
Q65 Newts in the pondCapture–recapture–Cross-sectional survey
Monitoring / surveillance		Photograph each newt's unique belly pattern, then net again: the share of already-seen newts in the second catch estimates the total (Lincoln–Petersen). It assumes no newts arrive or leave between visits and that every newt is equally likely to be caught. Watch out for: Selection bias.

Cross-sectional survey		Study one bounded case in depth, using documents, data, interviews and observation. Compare it with a sister plant that didn't improve; otherwise you are only studying the survivor. Watch out for: Survivorship bias.
Q58 Back to workGrounded theoryInterviews & thematic analysisProspective cohort
Systematic review & meta-analysis		'No theory yet' is the signature of grounded theory: iterative interviewing and coding, and theoretical sampling of new cases to test emerging concepts, until a model of the decision process emerges. Watch out for: Thin data.

Randomized controlled trial		An explanatory sequential design: use the survey to pick contrasting clinics (high and low adopters), interview staff to explain the numbers, then integrate the two strands. Watch out for: Selection bias.

Q06 Smog atlasEcological studyCross-sectional surveyProspective cohort
Randomized controlled trial		Comparing aggregate data across countries is the cheapest hypothesis generator there is. Just never conclude that polluted individuals get asthma from it: that's the ecological fallacy. Watch out for: Ecological fallacy.
Q07 Rare cancer, common sprayCase–control studyRetrospective cohortProspective cohort
Cross-sectional survey		Rare outcome: start from the cases. Draw controls from the population that produced them, and take exposure from records rather than memory, because grieving parents remember differently. A record-linkage cohort works only if registries already capture the pesticide exposure. Watch out for: Recall bias.
Q08 Clean-room workersRetrospective cohortProspective cohortCase–control study
Ecological study		Rare exposure: sample on the exposure. Employment records let you assemble the exposed workforce and an unexposed comparison group (e.g. office staff) and trace their outcomes. Comparing with the general public invites the healthy-worker effect. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q09 Snow shovelCase-crossoverCase–control studyCross-sectional survey
Prospective cohort		Brief exposure, acute effect: compare each patient's exertion in the hour before their heart attack with their own usual exertion. Every stable personal confounder cancels out. Watch out for: Recall bias.
Q10 Diet and dementiaProspective cohortRetrospective cohortRandomized controlled trial
Case–control study		Measure diet before anyone has dementia and wait, or (faster) analyse an existing cohort that recorded diet decades ago. Watch for reverse causation: early, pre-clinical dementia changes eating habits. Watch out for: Reverse causation.
Q66 Migraine genesGenome-wide association studyCase–control studyMendelian randomization
Case report / case series		Test every variant across the genome against a threshold of about 5 × 10⁻⁸, so that millions of tests don't produce false hits. Adjust for ancestry so population structure doesn't fake associations, and replicate the hits in an independent cohort. Watch out for: Forking paths & p-hacking.
Q67 The freezerCase-cohort / nested case–controlCase–control studyProspective cohort
Retrospective cohort		Test all 900 cases plus a random subcohort drawn from the whole cohort, blind to who is a case and in random order, so lab batches can't line up with the outcome. The same subcohort can later be reused for other outcomes. Watch out for: Measurement error.

Q11 New pill vs. oldRandomized controlled trialAdaptive / platform trialRetrospective cohort
Case report / case series		A clean causal question about an intervention you can assign, with genuine uncertainty about which is better: randomize. Conceal allocation, blind if you can, analyse by intention to treat. Watch out for: Attrition & missing data.
Q12 Peer tutoringCluster randomized trialStepped-wedge trialRandomized controlled trialWhen an intervention spreads between individuals, randomize the group (class or school) instead. Then analyse at the right level, because pupils in the same class aren't independent. Watch out for: Unit-of-analysis error.
Q13 Soda taxDifference-in-differencesSynthetic control, Interrupted time seriesCross-sectional surveyCompare the change in the taxed state with the change in the untaxed one. The key assumption, parallel trends, can be checked in the years before the tax. Watch out for: History & secular trends.
Q14 Scholarship cutoffRegression discontinuity–Retrospective cohort
Cross-sectional survey		A student who scored 70 and one who scored 69 are nearly identical, except one got the money. The estimate is local to the cutoff. Check that nobody could nudge their score across it. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q15 Sepsis alertInterrupted time seriesDifference-in-differencesCross-sectional survey
Case report / case series		Many time points before and after a sharp date separate the alert's effect from the trend that was already under way. Add a control series (another hospital, or a condition the alert can't affect) to rule out coincident changes. Watch out for: History & secular trends.
Q16 ReunificationSynthetic controlInterrupted time seriesRandomized controlled trial
Cross-sectional survey		One treated unit, many possible comparisons: build a weighted blend of other OECD countries that tracked West Germany before 1990, then watch where the paths split. Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller did exactly this. Watch out for: History & secular trends.
Q17 Her migraineN-of-1 trialCrossover trialRandomized controlled trial
Case report / case series		Frequent attacks and a fast-acting drug: randomize which drug she takes for each attack (or block of attacks), blind it with identical capsules, and repeat. The answer is about her. Watch out for: Expectation effects.
Q18 Two inhalersCrossover trialRandomized controlled trialRetrospective cohortStable chronic condition, short-acting reversible effect: each person tries both in random order with a washout. Between-person noise vanishes, so far fewer participants are needed. Watch out for: Carryover.
Q19 Three knobsField experiment, Factorial experiment (DoE)–Cross-sectional survey
Case study (in-depth)		Cross the three factors in a factorial layout, randomized to plots in blocks across several farms. One season yields main effects and interactions. This is Fisher's original idea. Watch out for: Limited generalizability.
Q20 Weld strengthFactorial experiment (DoE)Computational simulationRetrospective cohort
Case report / case series		A fractional-factorial screening design tests six factors in a few dozen runs and reveals interactions that one-factor-at-a-time testing misses. Randomize the run order to protect against drift. Simulation helps only if a validated process model exists. Watch out for: Measurement error.
Q21 Checkout buttonOnline A/B testRandomized controlled trialInterrupted time series
Interviews & thematic analysis		Randomize visitors to the old or new button and compare conversion. Fix the sample size and the metric in advance: peeking until it's significant is the classic A/B-test sin. Watch out for: Forking paths & p-hacking.
Q22 Rat-free islandsField experimentDifference-in-differencesCross-sectional survey
Case report / case series		Randomly choose which 10 islands get eradication and measure all 20 before and after (a randomized BACI design). Random allocation stops you from picking the 'easy' islands. Twenty units is few, so plan the power. Watch out for: Low power.
Q23 Triage rolloutStepped-wedge trialCluster randomized trial, Interrupted time seriesRandomized controlled trialEveryone gets it; only the order is open. Randomize the order in which hospitals switch, and every hospital contributes both control and intervention time. Model the background time trend carefully. Watch out for: History & secular trends.
Q24 Ten candidatesAdaptive / platform trialRandomized controlled trialCase report / case series
Retrospective cohort		One master protocol, one shared control arm; arms are dropped for futility and new ones added. RECOVERY found that dexamethasone saves lives within about 100 days of launch. Watch out for: History & secular trends.
Q25 Older than the trialsTarget trial emulationRetrospective cohortCross-sectional survey
Case report / case series		Write the protocol of the trial you'd like to run (eligibility, treatment strategies, time zero, follow-up) and emulate it in the claims data with new users and an active comparator. This designs out immortal-time and prevalent-user bias; what remains is confounding by indication. Watch out for: Confounding by indication.
Q26 Does school pay?Instrumental variable analysisRegression discontinuityCross-sectional surveyQuarter of birth nudges schooling but (arguably) affects earnings only through schooling, which makes it an instrument. Interrogate that 'only through' assumption hard, and check the instrument isn't weak. Watch out for: Confounding.
Q27 LDL: cause or marker?Mendelian randomizationRandomized controlled trialCross-sectional survey
Ecological study		People inherit LDL-raising or LDL-lowering variants at conception, essentially at random: a natural lifelong trial. Large genetic consortia make it fast. Pleiotropy (genes acting through other paths) is the threat to check. Trials of LDL-lowering drugs answer a shorter-term version. Watch out for: Confounding.

Q28 Who crashes next?Prediction model (statistical / ML)Benchmark evaluationCase–control study
Randomized controlled trial		Develop on one period, then validate on a later period and at another hospital. Guard ruthlessly against leakage, e.g. a lab test that was ordered because the doctor already suspected deterioration. Watch out for: Overfitting & leakage.
Q29 Spring floodPrediction model (statistical / ML)Computational simulationField experiment
Case study (in-depth)		Decades of snowpack and inflow records support a statistical forecast; a physically based hydrological simulation is a strong alternative or complement. Judge either one by its skill on years it never saw. Watch out for: Overfitting & leakage.
Q30 Next superconductorPrediction model (statistical / ML)Computational simulationFactorial experiment (DoE)
Systematic review & meta-analysis		Train a model on known materials to rank the candidates, optionally refine the shortlist with first-principles simulation, then test the top hits. Report performance on genuinely new chemistries, not near-duplicates of the training set. Watch out for: Overfitting & leakage.
Q31 Biomarker add-onPrediction model (statistical / ML)Prospective cohortCase–control study
Randomized controlled trial		Association isn't prediction. Compare the model with and without the biomarker on discrimination, calibration and net benefit, in data the model hasn't seen. Watch out for: Overfitting & leakage.
Q32 Range in 2070Prediction model (statistical / ML)Computational simulationCase study (in-depth)
Randomized controlled trial		Species distribution models link today's occurrences to climate, then project them onto scenarios. Mechanistic (physiological) simulations are a valuable cross-check when the future climate falls outside anything in the training data. Watch out for: Limited generalizability.
Q69 Worth the price?Cost-effectiveness analysisComputational simulationRandomized controlled trial
Systematic review & meta-analysis		Build a decision model of who catches RSV, who is admitted to hospital and who dies, with and without vaccination. Feed it trial efficacy, costs and quality of life, and report the extra cost per QALY gained, with a probabilistic sensitivity analysis to show how certain that answer is. Watch out for: Unvalidated model.

Q33 Rapid strepDiagnostic accuracy study–Case–control study
Randomized controlled trial		Recruit consecutive children with sore throats, do both tests on everyone, and read each test blind to the other. Sensitivity and specificity only mean something in the population where the test will be used. Watch out for: Spectrum bias.
Q34 Burnout scaleInstrument development & validationMixed methods (sequential)Cross-sectional survey
Systematic review & meta-analysis		Generate items from theory and interviews, refine them with experts and cognitive testing, then test factor structure, reliability and validity in fresh samples. An exploratory sequential mixed-methods design is essentially this. Watch out for: Wrong construct.
Q35 Two readersReliability & agreement study–Diagnostic accuracy study
Cross-sectional survey		Both read the same representative set of images, independently and blind to each other; quantify agreement with weighted kappa. The images must span the full range of severity. Watch out for: Spectrum bias.
Q36 Wrist vs. cuffReliability & agreement studyDiagnostic accuracy studyEcological study
Randomized controlled trial		This is a method-comparison study: measure the same people with both devices and compute Bland–Altman limits of agreement. Decide in advance what difference is clinically acceptable, and don't trust correlation: one device could read 15 mmHg high every time and still correlate perfectly. Include people across the whole blood-pressure range. Watch out for: Spectrum bias.
Q37 Lead in the waterInterlaboratory comparisonReliability & agreement studySystematic review & meta-analysis
Monitoring / surveillance		Send identical, homogeneous samples (ideally with a certified reference value) to every lab, and quantify repeatability and reproducibility (ISO 5725). Watch out for: Measurement error.
Q64 Grit or not?Nomological network studyInstrument development & validationProspective cohort
Reliability & agreement study		Write down the predicted pattern before collecting data: grit should correlate with conscientiousness, but not so highly that they are the same thing, and it should predict outcomes beyond it. Measure both traits by more than one method (self-report, teacher ratings, behaviour), so shared questionnaire habits don't fake the overlap. Watch out for: Measurement error.
Q68 Seeds of choiceDiscrete choice experimentMixed methods (sequential)Cross-sectional survey
Randomized controlled trial		Offer choices between seed packages whose yield, price, taste and saveability vary by an experimental design; choice models turn the answers into the weight of each feature and a willingness to pay. Pick the attributes with farmers first, and remember that stated choices overstate real purchases. Watch out for: Limited generalizability.

Q38 Engine limitAnalytical / mathematical theory–Factorial experiment (DoE)
Computational simulation		'Any engine' is a universal claim, and only a derivation from the laws of thermodynamics can establish it. Carnot's bound came from reasoning, not measurement. Watch out for: Unvalidated model.
Q39 Bridge in a stormComputational simulationFactorial experiment (DoE)Case report / case series
Cross-sectional survey		Coupled fluid–structure simulation lets you push the virtual bridge to failure. Validate the model against wind-tunnel tests of scale models (the 'ok' answer) before trusting the extreme cases. Watch out for: Unvalidated model.
Q40 School closuresComputational simulationInterrupted time seriesRandomized controlled trial
Cross-sectional survey		An agent-based model with households, schools and workplaces lets you play out scenarios. Present results as ranges under varied assumptions, and check them against data from past epidemics. Watch out for: Unvalidated model.
Q41 Clock in a cellAnalytical / mathematical theoryComputational simulationCross-sectional survey
Case–control study		Write the dynamics as differential equations and analyse their stability; the conditions for oscillation fall out of the mathematics. Simulation explores the parameter space, and the lab then builds and tests the circuit. Watch out for: Unvalidated model.
Q42 Neutron-star crashComputational simulationAnalytical / mathematical theoryCase report / case series
Factorial experiment (DoE)		The equations are known but have no closed-form solution in this regime, so numerical-relativity simulations produce the waveform templates that detectors search for. Approximate analytical models cover the early inspiral. Watch out for: Unvalidated model.
Q43 Price warAnalytical / mathematical theoryComputational simulationCase study (in-depth)
Online A/B test		Game theory derives equilibria, and the conditions for them, from stated assumptions. The answer is only as good as those assumptions: state them explicitly and test how robust the result is. Watch out for: Unvalidated model.
Q70 Why this deal?Process tracingCase study (in-depth)Ecological study
Cross-sectional survey		Write down what each explanation predicts you should find (who met when, what was offered, what the leadership said in private), then look for hoop tests that can eliminate an explanation and smoking guns that strongly confirm one. Weigh evidence against your favourite account as hard as evidence for it. Watch out for: Researcher bias.

Q44 Grasp benchmarkBenchmark evaluationFactorial experiment (DoE)Case report / case series
Interviews & thematic analysis		Same objects, same robot, same metrics and published baselines, plus ablations and repeated trials with confidence intervals. Report the failures, and test beyond the benchmark: sim-to-real gaps are where claims die. Watch out for: Forking paths & p-hacking.
Q45 A hand for kidsDesign science (build & evaluate)Mixed methods (sequential), Pilot / feasibility studyRandomized controlled trial
Systematic review & meta-analysis		Iterate: understand the needs, prototype, test with users, refine. Evaluation is built into every cycle, and later the finished device can face a trial. Watch out for: Researcher bias.
Q46 Review workbenchDesign science (build & evaluate)Mixed methods (sequential), Pilot / feasibility studyRandomized controlled trial
Case report / case series		Build in short cycles with real researchers, and evaluate each version on real tasks: usability, time taken, and completeness of the resulting review. You are not a neutral judge of your own tool, so let others test it. Watch out for: Researcher bias.
Q47 Will they come?Pilot / feasibility study–Randomized controlled trial
Systematic review & meta-analysis		A feasibility study measures recruitment, retention, adherence and data quality against pre-set progression criteria. Resist reporting its effect estimates: it isn't powered for them. Watch out for: Low power.
Q48 Therapy over a bad linePilot / feasibility studyDesign science (build & evaluate), Mixed methods (sequential)Randomized controlled trial
Ecological study		Feasibility first: run it in a few clinics, measure uptake, completion and technical failures, and interview staff and patients about what got in the way. Watch out for: Limited generalizability.
Q61 The tangle clipPrior art reviewScoping reviewDesign science (build & evaluate)
Systematic review & meta-analysis		Novelty is judged against everything made public anywhere, in any language: patents, products, catalogues, theses and papers. Search patent databases by classification code as well as by keyword, because patents rarely use everyday words like 'clip'. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q72 The abandoned formUsability test (think-aloud)Design science (build & evaluate), Interviews & thematic analysisOnline A/B test
Cross-sectional survey		Ask five or so representative older users to book an appointment while thinking aloud; watch without helping and log every hesitation and error. Fix the worst problems and test again: several small rounds find more than one big one. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q73 No-showsPlan–Do–Study–Act cyclesInterrupted time seriesCluster randomized trial
Systematic review & meta-analysis		Run small cycles: plan a change, try it for a week or two, study the run chart, act on what you learn. Mark every change on the chart, and keep measuring long enough to tell real improvement from seasonal swings. Watch out for: History & secular trends.

Q49 Run it again?Systematic review & meta-analysis–Randomized controlled trial
Case study (in-depth)		Search exhaustively, appraise risk of bias, pool with a random-effects model and explore why the trials disagree. Look for missing small negative trials. Watch out for: Publication bias.
Q50 Plastic in the soilScoping review–Field experiment
Network meta-analysis		This is a mapping question: chart the literature systematically (PRISMA-ScR) without pooling effects. The output is a map of evidence and gaps that tells everyone what to study next. Watch out for: Publication bias.
Q51 Six pills, few duelsNetwork meta-analysisSystematic review & meta-analysisRandomized controlled trial
Retrospective cohort		Combine direct and indirect comparisons in one network to rank the treatments. Check that the trials are similar enough for indirect comparisons to be fair (transitivity). Watch out for: Apples & oranges.
Q52 Three weeksRapid reviewUmbrella reviewSystematic review & meta-analysis
Cluster randomized trial		Streamline a systematic review transparently: a narrower question, fewer databases, single screening with verification. Report what you cut, so readers can judge. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q53 Vitamin D for everythingUmbrella review–Randomized controlled trial
Case report / case series		Review the reviews: grade each claimed association by its size and credibility, and watch for overlapping primary studies being counted twice. Watch out for: Apples & oranges.
Q54 Works here, fails thereRealist reviewSystematic review & meta-analysis, Mixed methods (sequential)Randomized controlled trial
Ecological study		Realist synthesis asks how context triggers mechanisms that produce outcomes, drawing on trials, process evaluations and qualitative studies alike. Watch out for: Apples & oranges.
Q62 Roots of a fieldCo-citation analysisBibliographic couplingSystematic review & meta-analysis
Delphi consensus		Two works are linked each time a later paper cites both, so clusters of co-cited works are the field's schools of thought, and the works that bridge clusters show where ecology and economics meet. Normalize for how often each work is cited, and remember that a citation isn't an endorsement. Watch out for: Wrong construct.
Q63 Paper floodBibliographic couplingScoping reviewCo-citation analysis
Systematic review & meta-analysis		Papers that cite the same sources are probably working on the same problem, and that link exists the day a paper is published. Down-weight references that nearly everyone cites (here, the classic deep-learning papers), or they glue every paper into one blob. Then name each cluster by reading its core papers. Watch out for: Wrong construct.
Q71 Under fiftyIndividual participant data meta-analysisSystematic review & meta-analysisNetwork meta-analysis
Retrospective cohort		Ask every trial team for its patient-level data, check it, and analyse age the same way in every trial: ideally as a continuous variable, with the treatment-by-age interaction estimated within each trial. Report which trials couldn't share data and whether their results differ. Watch out for: Selection bias.
Q74 The blogs know moreMultivocal literature reviewScoping review, Delphi consensusSystematic review & meta-analysis
Randomized controlled trial		When practice runs ahead of research, a multivocal review follows Garousi et al. (2019): search academic databases and the grey literature (Google, practitioner sites, conference talks), grade each grey source for authority, method and objectivity, stop by saturation, and report the white and grey evidence side by side. Watch out for: Unvetted sources.
